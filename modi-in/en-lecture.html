A mission everyone called hard to crack, maybe even impossible

After the Battle of Sultan Yacoub in June 1982, three IDF soldiers remained in enemy territory for decades, and no one knew what had become of them.

Modi Ben Shaul led the research to locate them for eight years. With no complete picture, facing partial and contradictory information, several research bodies each holding a different piece, and people who were sure there was nothing left to look for.

The keynote opens the file: how to build a situational picture from small pieces, what to filter out and how, how to keep a team going for years without an answer, and how to keep looking for a way when others before you did not succeed.

And from there, straight to the desks of the employees and managers in the audience: exactly the same questions come up in every organization facing a hard decision, limited resources and incomplete information.

Glossary

Tools from the intelligence world

Concepts that come up in the keynote

Devil’s advocate

Ipcha Mistabra

“The opposite is likely.” Deliberately testing the scenario opposite to the accepted assessment, so you don’t lock onto a single explanation.

Devil’s advocate

Competing hypotheses

ACH · Method

A method developed by Richards Heuer of the CIA: raise several possible explanations for the same evidence, and look for the one that the least evidence contradicts.

Competing hypotheses

Rumsfeld matrix

Known unknowns

Four kinds of knowledge: what we know we know, what we know we don’t know, what we don’t know we don’t know, and what we know without realizing it.

The Rumsfeld matrix

Facts vs. assessments

Analytic thinking

A ground rule of intelligence analysis: separate what is known from what is estimated, and go back to the original raw data when an old assessment starts behaving like a fact.

Nine tools from intelligence

Open-source intelligence

OSINT · Core concept

Collecting and analyzing information that has been published openly and legally: media, social networks, websites and official publications. The value is never in a single piece of information, but in cross-checking many of them.
